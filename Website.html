ReWater: the problem

Office greywater is discharged while toilets and irrigation use treated mains water. The barrier is the engineering and business case: treatment, pumping energy, maintenance, CAPEX and OPEX must be balanced together.

Core model: mass-balance and hydraulic digital twin, supply/demand forecasting and constrained optimisation, guarded by rule-based safety interlocks.

AquaWise: the problem

Residents see only their bills while operators get readings from different meters and periods. Unusual demand and campaign results stay hidden, so decisions are reactive.

Core model: time-series demand forecasting plus consumption benchmarking and a recommendation engine.

Start with a transparent physics model (mass balance, pump affinity laws, Darcy–Weisbach losses) and rule-based control. Add model-predictive control and machine-learning forecasts once data exists. Forecasts are scored with MAE or MAPE on a held-out period and every result is compared with a fixed-rate baseline.

Sense

Flow, level, turbidity, pH, residual chlorine, membrane pressure, kWh every 1–15 min.

Validate

Time-stamp, range-check, reconcile. Gaps and drift are flagged, never treated as zero.

Model

Digital twin: mass balance, tanks, pump curves, pipe losses, membrane fouling trend.

Forecast

Hourly greywater supply and non-potable demand from occupancy, weekday and calendar.

Optimise

Pick throughput, pump speed, backwash and dosing that minimise cost within limits.

Act and learn

Send setpoints to PLC and drives; compare forecast with actual; update model.

Control decisions (illustrative, to tune on pilot data)

  • Weekend wind-down: cut throughput and air-scour; tank covers flushing; restart before Monday peak.
  • Flush-peak pre-fill: fill the treated tank before peaks so the booster runs slower.
  • Rain-event priority: use rainwater first, hold greywater within hold-time limit.
  • Fouling response: if permeability falls faster than trend, clean earlier and reduce flux.

ReWater · Mass balance and energy

Qcollected = Qreused + Qrejected + Qoverflow + ΔStorage. Defaults reproduce the case-study basis (Habibullah et al., 2023).

Daily balance

Overflow and storage change assumed zero in this view.

GBI WE2 progress (proxy: treated ÷ recorded use)

1 point at ≥10%, 2 points at ≥30%. GBI's own methodology governs; this is a consumption proxy.

Pump affinity laws: why variable speed saves energy

Ideal centrifugal pump: flow ∝ speed, power ∝ speed³. Static head reduces the saving. P = ρgQH/η.
Worked example and formulas
  • Collected: 270.9 + 249.7 = 520.6 m³ in 60 days = 8.7 m³/day (Habibullah et al., 2023).
  • Greywater share of recorded use: 520.6 ÷ 2,676.9 = 19.4%.
  • Reusable water: 8.7 × 0.90 × 365 ≈ 2,860 m³/year (the proposal rounds to 2,850 from 7.8 m³/day).
  • Treated share of recorded use ≈ 7.8 ÷ 44.6 = 17.5%, above the 10% GBI tier; the 30% tier needs about 1.7× the volume.
  • Recovery (90%), specific energy (1.0 kWh/m³) and hold time are planning assumptions, to be replaced by metered data.
  • An unexplained balance gap triggers a meter-fault and leak check.

ReWater · Safety, risk and scorecard

Rule-based interlocks, not AI, make safety decisions.

Interlock logic: if turbidity, pH or chlorine residual leaves limits, a sensor fails, or tank level is abnormal → divert to sewer, stop reuse, switch non-potable demand to mains top-up.

Pilots, roadmap and evidence

Pilot figures are proposed evaluation measures, not verified savings.

Innovation

A control layer that optimises treatment, pumping and reuse demand together, hardware-agnostic. AquaWise adds forecasting and scorecards.

Impact

Mains water, energy, cost, GBI WE2 credits and ESG reporting, tracked per m³.

Feasibility

Packaged-unit pilot, a measured Malaysian baseline and rule-based safety interlocks.

Honesty

Every figure is tagged quoted calculated assumption or synthetic.

Scalability

One building, then a portfolio, then township zones with role-based reporting.
